# Writeup

**How I matched locations.** I matched each website location to the CRM by street address and city, not by name. Names change when a facility rebrands or gets bought, but the building stays where it is. Names can also mislead. One Bellhaven name shows up in Colorado for a facility that has nothing to do with Bellhaven. If the address didn't match, I only accepted a match when the city was the same, the names were very close, and the phone or administrator also lined up. A name by itself was never enough. One community was hidden from the directory page and only linked from the homepage, so the scraper follows homepage links too. It also stops if it finds fewer locations than the site says it has.

**The tough calls.** Marietta and Tiffin moved to Bellhaven, but their old accounts still had revenue and unpaid AR. The billing SOP says to leave those accounts alone, so I created new accounts under Bellhaven and linked the old ones to them. Lima, Findlay, Zanesville, and Kettering had no AR, so I just moved them under Bellhaven. When I found duplicates, I kept the account with billing history first, then the one with more contacts, then the more complete record. Sandusky was the hardest one. It had revenue and AR, and a Millstone account already existed at the same address. Instead of making another duplicate, I linked the old account to that Millstone account. Alliance and Coldwater are no longer on the website, but that doesn't prove they closed, so I marked them Needs Review.

**How I used AI.** I used Claude Code to build this. I set the rules, decided what mattered most, and reviewed every change before it went into the CRM. I didn't take its output at face value. When I counted the data by hand early on, I caught a bug that made 27 locations look brand new. I also had it build a separate checker that doesn't use the matching code. It compares the final CRM to a snapshot I took before making any changes. The tests also break a correct result on purpose to make sure the checker catches it.

**Result.** I reviewed and approved all 30 proposed changes. The checker passed all 11 of its checks on the live CRM. When I ran the sync a second time, it found nothing new to change.

**What I'd build next.** Slack alerts when there are new changes to review, syncing administrators into the contacts list, and running the same process for every parent company, not just Bellhaven.
